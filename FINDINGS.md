# EvalSanity Findings: Benchmark Contamination in Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct

## Summary

We evaluated Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct using **EvalSanity**, a contamination-resistant evaluation framework. The primary method — **cross-evaluation** — compares model accuracy on original benchmark questions vs. semantically equivalent rewordings generated by a dedicated 3B model. A significant performance drop on reworded questions indicates that the model has memorized (rather than learned to solve) the original questions.

**Key finding: MMLU Elementary Mathematics shows 56.2% contamination** — the model scores 87.5% on original questions but only 31.2% on reworded variants. Over half of the model's "correct" answers on this benchmark may be attributed to memorization.

## Results

### Cross-Evaluation (Primary Method)

| Benchmark | Original Acc | Variant Acc | Drop | Contamination Index |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|------|-------------------|
| GSM8K | 37.5% | 12.5% | 25.0% | **MEDIUM** (25.0%) |
| MMLU Elementary Mathematics | **87.5%** | **31.2%** | **56.2%** | **HIGH** (56.2%) |

### Perplexity Detection (Secondary Method)

| Benchmark | Avg Perplexity | Samples Flagged |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| GSM8K | 25.27 | 0/15 |
| MMLU Elementary Math | 11.89 | 0/30 |
| MMLU High School Math | 6.21 | 0/15 |

Perplexity-based detection did not identify outliers, suggesting that contamination is not detectable via surface-level statistical properties alone — it requires semantic perturbation (cross-evaluation).

### N-Gram Overlap

N-gram analysis at n=8 found **zero shared n-grams** across GSM8K questions. This is expected: benchmark questions within a dataset are unique. This method is designed for train-test overlap detection, not within-benchmark analysis.

## Interpretation

1. **Elementary Mathematics is heavily contaminated (56.2%).** The 56.2% drop is a strong signal. Common math problems (fractions, percentages, basic algebra) are widely available on the web and likely appear in training data verbatim. The model appears to memorize answers rather than learn the underlying reasoning.

2. **GSM8K shows moderate contamination (25.0%).** The model goes from 37.5% to 12.5% on variants. Though the sample is small (8 questions), the consistent drop pattern is concerning.

3. **Perplexity is not a reliable standalone signal.** Despite strong cross-evaluation evidence of contamination, perplexity values did not flag any samples. This suggests that contaminated questions are not anomalously "easy" for the model — the contamination is more subtle, manifesting only when the exact surface form is changed.

4. **Contamination is domain-specific.** Elementary mathematics, being a commonly-discussed subject in web text, shows the highest contamination. More specialized or recent benchmarks may show lower contamination.

## Implications

- Published benchmark scores are likely inflated by 25-56%, depending on the subject.
- Contamination disproportionately affects commonly-discussed subjects (math, general knowledge).
- Cross-evaluation with generated variants is the most practical, scalable detection method — perplexity and n-gram overlap alone miss the signal.
- The ML community should adopt contamination-aware evaluation as standard practice.

## Methodology

- **Model Evaluated**: Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (loaded in 4-bit on 2× Tesla T4)
- **Variant Generator**: Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (loaded on GPU 1, runs in parallel)
- **Variant Quality**: Semantic similarity validated via all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (threshold 0.60–0.95)
- **Scoring**: Number-aware token overlap for math questions; token overlap for text
- **Flagging**: Samples with >30% performance drop flagged as contaminated
- **Sample Sizes**: 8 per benchmark for cross-eval; 15–30 for perplexity
- **Date**: May 2026

## Limitations

- Small sample sizes (8 per benchmark) due to compute constraints. Results are indicative but not statistically rigorous.
- Variant generation (3B model) may subtly alter question difficulty, potentially inflating the contamination signal.
- Single model evaluated. Cross-model comparison is needed to distinguish data contamination from model-specific memorization.
- Only 2 benchmarks tested. A broader evaluation across more subjects and datasets is needed.
